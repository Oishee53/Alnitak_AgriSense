# Vegetable Production Guide: Tomato, Brinjal — Bangladesh
Source: compiled from BARI vegetable research guides and DAE IPM leaflets (public extension materials). High-value crops with strong demand, but heavy labour, pest pressure, and price crashes at seasonal glut.

## Tomato — season, soil, water
Rabi crop. Raise seedlings in a nursery, transplant mid-October to end-November; first harvest ~90 days, picking through ~120 days. Best on well-drained loam with staking; steady moisture but never waterlogged. Winter is the main crop; summer/heat-tolerant hybrids exist but are harder.

## Tomato — yield, fertilizer, economics
BARI Tomato-14, -15 and many hybrids. Yield 20–30 t/ha, about 215–320 maund/acre; planning figure 250. Split urea plus full TSP, MoP, gypsum, zinc, boron; stake and tie plants. Peak-season prices can crash — sell staggered, and grade fruit for better rates.

## Tomato — risks
Late blight in foggy weather (prophylactic mancozeb), whitefly-borne tomato yellow leaf curl virus (TYLCV), and fruit borer. Price crash at glut is the main financial risk.

## Brinjal (Begun) — season, soil, water
Grows year-round; main crops transplanted in Rabi and late Kharif (mid-September to mid-November). Long harvest window (~90 to 150+ days) gives steady cash flow. Loam and silt-loam best; steady moisture, tolerant of both winter and monsoon with drainage.

## Brinjal — yield, fertilizer, economics
BARI Begun varieties (and Bt brinjal lines for borer resistance). Yield 20–25 t/ha, about 215–270 maund/acre; planning figure 220. Split urea through the long harvest plus full TSP, MoP, gypsum, zinc, boron.

## Brinjal — risks
Brinjal shoot and fruit borer is the dominant pest — without IPM (pheromone traps, weekly clipping of infested shoots) or Bt varieties it drives very heavy pesticide use. Bacterial wilt in waterlogged soil. Choose IPM to cut cost and residues.
