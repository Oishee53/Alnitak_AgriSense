# Spice Crops Production Guide: Onion, Garlic, Chili — Bangladesh
Source: compiled from BARI spice research guides and DAE crop calendars (public extension materials). These are high-value, high-cost, high-risk crops; strong profit potential but heavy working capital and price volatility.

## Onion (Peyaj) — season, soil, water
Rabi crop. Transplant 40–45 day seedlings November to mid-December; harvest March–April. Duration ~110 days. Best on well-drained loam and silt-loam; shallow-rooted, so it needs frequent light irrigation and cannot tolerate drought or waterlogging. Not suited to heavy clay.

## Onion — yield, fertilizer, economics
BARI Peyaj-1, -4, -5. Yield 8–12 t/ha, about 90–130 maund/acre; planning figure 90. High cost from seed/sets and heavy labour. Fertilizer: split urea plus full TSP, MoP, gypsum, zinc, boron; stop irrigation before harvest and cure bulbs in shade. Onion prices swing violently between harvest glut and lean season — storage or staggered selling matters.

## Onion — risks
Purple blotch and thrips; severe post-harvest price crashes; storage rot. The biggest risk is financial (price), not agronomic.

## Garlic (Rosun) — season, soil, water
Rabi crop. Plant cloves late October to late November; harvest March. Duration ~140 days. Often grown zero-till (dibbled into soft moist soil after Aman), which cuts land-prep cost. Loam and silt-loam best; steady moisture with light irrigations.

## Garlic — yield, fertilizer, economics
BARI Rosun-1, -3, -4. Yield ~6 t/ha, about 60 maund/acre. Seed cloves are the dominant cost (very expensive), so garlic needs high working capital. Mulch with straw to conserve moisture and suppress weeds. Split urea + TSP, MoP, gypsum, zinc, boron.

## Garlic — risks
High seed-clove cost, leaf blight/purple blotch in humid spells, and storage loss.

## Chili (Morich) — season, soil, water
Transplanted mostly in the Rabi window (mid-October to end-November), harvested over a long period from ~110 days onward. Sensitive to waterlogging — needs ridges/beds and good drainage; steady moisture at fruiting. Loam and sandy-loam best.

## Chili — yield, fertilizer, economics
BARI Morich-1, -3. Dry-chili yield ~1.5–2 t/ha, about 15–18 maund/acre; green chili yields more by weight. High labour for picking. Split urea in three plus full TSP, MoP, gypsum, zinc, boron. Dry red chili stores well and fetches a high price.

## Chili — risks
Anthracnose fruit rot in wet weather, thrips/mite-induced leaf curl, and sharp price swings. High reward but high risk — not ideal for a farmer who cannot absorb a bad-price year.
