# Maize Production Guide (Kharif and Rabi) — Bangladesh
Source: compiled from DAE maize production guide, BARC FRG-2018, BARI hybrid maize recommendations (public extension materials).

## Seasons and sowing windows
Maize grows in two seasons in Bangladesh. Rabi (winter) maize: sow mid-October to end-November, harvest March-April — highest yields. Kharif maize: sow mid-June to end-July with the monsoon, harvest October — slightly lower yield but frees Rabi land. Kharif sowing after early August risks flowering in heavy September rain.

## Soil suitability
Well-drained loam and sandy-loam soils are best. Maize tolerates sandier soil than rice but is very sensitive to waterlogging — even 2 days of standing water at knee-high stage can kill the crop. In Kharif, choose higher land and make drainage furrows every 4 rows. Heavy clay with poor drainage is unsuitable in monsoon.

## Recommended varieties
Hybrids dominate: NK40, Pioneer 3396, Miracle, BARI Hybrid Maize-9 and -16. Seed rate 8–9 kg per acre, spacing 60 × 20 cm, one plant per hill after thinning.

## Water management
Kharif maize is largely rainfed; drainage matters more than irrigation. Critical stress-free stages: knee-high (V8), tasseling-silking (55–65 DAS) and grain filling. If a dry spell exceeds 10 days at tasseling, irrigate once (~50 mm). Rabi maize needs 3–4 irrigations.

## Typical yield
Hybrid maize yields 7–9 t/ha in Rabi (75–95 maund/acre) and 5.5–7 t/ha in Kharif (about 60–75 maund/acre). Planning figure for Kharif on loam: 75 maund/acre.

## Market and use
Strong steady demand from the poultry and fish feed industry; price less volatile than potato. Grain must be dried to 14% moisture; moldy grain (aflatoxin) is rejected by feed mills.

## Major risks
Fall armyworm is the main pest — scout whorls weekly from 20 DAS, hand-crush egg masses, spray spinetoram or emamectin benzoate only past 10% infested plants. Waterlogging in Kharif. Lodging in nor'wester storms if over-fertilized with urea.
