# Transplanted Aman (T. Aman) Rice Production Guide — Bangladesh
Source: compiled from BRRI "Adhunik Dhaner Chash", DAE crop calendar, BARC FRG-2018 (public extension materials).

## Season and sowing window
T. Aman is the main monsoon (Kharif-2) rice crop of Bangladesh. Seedbed sowing runs mid-June to mid-July; transplanting runs 15 July to 15 August. Transplanting after end-August reduces yield sharply because short days force early flowering. The crop matures in about 110–120 days after transplanting and is harvested in November to early December.

## Soil suitability
Best on clay and clay-loam soils that hold standing water; loam is good; silty soils acceptable. Light sandy soils are poor for T. Aman because they cannot hold puddled water, causing drought stress between rains. Medium-high to medium-low land is ideal; deeply flooded land needs submergence-tolerant varieties (BRRI dhan51, BRRI dhan52).

## Recommended varieties
BRRI dhan49 (135 days, popular), BRRI dhan87 (127 days, high yield), BRRI dhan75 (short duration, premium aroma), BRRI dhan51/52 (submergence tolerant), Swarna (local favourite, blast-prone). Short-duration varieties (BRRI dhan75, dhan87) free the field early for Rabi mustard or potato.

## Seed and seedling management
Seed rate 10–12 kg per acre for transplanting. Use 25–30 day old seedlings, 2–3 seedlings per hill, spacing 20 × 15 cm. Older seedlings (>35 days) tiller poorly and yield less.

## Water management
T. Aman is mainly rainfed. Normal monsoon rainfall (200–350 mm per month, July–September) meets most water need. Keep 2–5 cm standing water from tillering to grain filling. If a dry spell (>10 rain-free days) hits at panicle initiation or flowering, one supplemental irrigation of about 50 mm protects yield. Drain the field 10–15 days before harvest.

## Typical yield
National average paddy yield is about 2.6–3.0 t/ha (26–30 maund/acre for older varieties); with modern varieties and good management 4.0–4.5 t/ha, i.e. roughly 40–48 maund per acre. A realistic planning figure on suitable soil is 45 maund/acre.

## Major risks
Flash flood and submergence in low land during July–August; drought spells in late September (affects flowering); stem borer, leaf folder, brown planthopper; bacterial leaf blight and blast. Avoid excess urea, which invites pests and lodging.
