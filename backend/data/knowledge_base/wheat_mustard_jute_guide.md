# Wheat, Mustard and Jute Production Notes — Bangladesh
Source: compiled from BWMRI wheat guide, BARI mustard guide, BJRI jute guide, DAE crop calendars (public extension materials).

## Wheat — season and window
Rabi crop. Sow 15 November – 10 December; every day of delay after 15 December costs roughly 1% yield because grain filling runs into March heat. Duration 100–110 days, harvest mid-March. Varieties: BARI Gom-30, -33 (blast tolerant), WMRI Gom-3.

## Wheat — soil, water, yield
Best on loam and silt-loam; tolerates lighter soil than rice. Needs only 2–3 irrigations: crown-root initiation at 20–25 DAS (the single most yield-critical irrigation), booting (~55 DAS), grain filling (~75 DAS). Yield 3.0–3.5 t/ha with modern varieties: about 28–33 maund/acre; planning figure 30.

## Wheat — risks
Wheat blast in warm humid February (avoid late sowing; use BARI Gom-33); rats at grain fill; heat shrivel if harvest slips past March.

## Mustard — season and window
Shortest Rabi crop (80–90 days) — fits between T. Aman harvest and Boro transplanting, turning a fallow window into oil income. Sow mid-October to mid-November on residual moisture. Varieties: BARI Sarisha-14 (75–80 days), BARI Sarisha-17, Tori-7 (local, low yield).

## Mustard — soil, water, yield
Loam and silt-loam best; grows on lighter soil than wheat. Often zero-irrigation on residual moisture; one light irrigation at flowering/siliqua fill lifts yield 20–25% in dry years. Yield 1.0–1.4 t/ha: 10–14 maund/acre; planning figure 12. Mustard is a strong choice when budget is tight — lowest input cost of the Rabi field crops.

## Mustard — risks
Aphid outbreaks in cool cloudy spells (December-January): spray only past 50 aphids/plant threshold. Waterlogging from unseasonal rain kills seedlings — ensure drainage.

## Jute — season and window
Kharif-1 fibre crop. Sow mid-March to end-April with pre-monsoon showers; harvest at 110–120 days (July-August) at small-pod stage for best fibre quality. Varieties: O-9897, BJRI Tossa Pat-8 (Robi-1).

## Jute — soil, water, yield
Loam and silt-loam of medium land best; needs clean, slow-moving retting water within reach at harvest — without retting water, fibre quality and price collapse. Fibre yield 2.0–2.5 t/ha: about 18–25 maund/acre; planning figure 20.

## Jute — risks
Hairy caterpillar and semilooper; late-monsoon retting water scarcity; fibre price swings with export demand. Labour need at harvest/retting is the highest of all field crops.
