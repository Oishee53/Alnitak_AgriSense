# Annual Crop Calendar Overview — Bangladesh
Source: compiled from DAE national crop calendar and BRRI/BARI season guides (public extension materials).

## What to plant by month
January: transplant Boro rice (till mid-Feb). February: sow mungbean (grishmokalin, from mid-Feb); sow spring groundnut/sugarcane; manage Boro/wheat/potato. March: sow jute (from mid-March), sow Kharif-1 aus rice, sow mungbean/groundnut. April: sow jute and aus rice (till end-April). May: prepare Aman seedbeds late-May. June: Aman seedbed sowing; sow Kharif maize (from mid-June). July: transplant T. Aman (from 15 July); last Kharif maize sowings; harvest aus rice. August: transplant T. Aman (till 15 August). September: no new field sowing; transplant brinjal; manage Aman. October: sow mustard, lentil and chickpea (from mid-Oct); transplant onion, chili, tomato; plant garlic and sweet potato; Rabi maize from mid-Oct. November: sow wheat (from 15 Nov), plant potato and garlic (from 15 Nov), plant onion, sow groundnut, finish mustard/lentil/chickpea sowing, plant sugarcane (autumn), Boro seedbed. December: finish wheat (by 10 Dec) and potato (by 15 Dec) planting; finish onion transplant and sweet potato; Boro seedbed care.

## Low-budget and sandy-soil options
For farmers with a tight budget, the pulses (lentil, chickpea, mungbean) have the lowest cash cost of the Rabi crops and fix soil nitrogen. For light sandy or char (riverbank) soils where rice and most crops struggle, groundnut and sweet potato are the best fits — both prefer sandy land and need little irrigation. High-value but high-cost/high-risk Rabi choices are potato, onion, garlic, chili, tomato and brinjal (heavy working capital and price/pest risk).

## Typical cropping patterns (NW Bangladesh)
Pattern 1 (rice-based): T. Aman (Jul–Nov) → Boro (Jan–May) → fallow. Pattern 2 (diversified, higher income): T. Aman (Jul–Nov) → Mustard (Oct/Nov–Jan, short) → Boro late or Maize Rabi. Pattern 3 (upland loam): Kharif maize (Jun–Oct) → Potato (Nov–Feb) → Boro/fallow. Short-duration Aman varieties (BRRI dhan75/87) make room for mustard or early potato.

## Decision guide for July–August (Aman window)
If land is medium/medium-low with clay-loam and monsoon is normal: T. Aman rice is the default choice. If land is higher, well-drained loam: Kharif maize competes well (feed demand, lower flood risk). If transplanting would slip past 15 August: switch to short-duration Aman variety or plan an early Rabi crop instead. Waterlogged low land with flash-flood history: submergence-tolerant BRRI dhan51/52 only.

## Decision guide for October–December (Rabi window)
Reliable irrigation + clay-loam: Boro rice (highest yield, highest cost). Limited water: wheat (2–3 irrigations) or mustard (0–2). High budget + loose loam + cold-storage access: potato (highest profit potential, highest risk). Tight budget after a poor Aman: mustard — lowest cash cost, 80-day turnaround.
